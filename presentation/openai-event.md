# The problem is not a lack of structure

Creative work may be distributed across:

- drafts and unfinished notes;
- posts, recordings, messages, and repositories;
- memory, association, and changing interpretation;
- tools that were never designed to preserve a whole creative history.

> A person can have a coherent body of work without being able to see all of its structure from inside it.

Speaker note: Replace the examples with ones your audience recognizes. Do not disclose private evidence.

# What gets lost

- When was this created rather than downloaded?
- Who made it, said it, uploaded it, or preserved it?
- Is this verified history, a creator statement, or an interpretation?
- How does it relate to the wider project?
- Does a connection imply collaboration, permission, or endorsement?
- Where should someone restart after an interruption?

---

# The design premise

Do not force a wandering mind into a rigid filing system.

Give every thought somewhere safe to land—and make returning easy.

Speaker note: Neurodivergent people are not a single audience. Present this as a design hypothesis to test with communities, not a universal description.

---

# The workflow

1. **Capture** what it is and where it came from.
2. **Preserve** originals, dates, authorship evidence, and versions.
3. **Classify** each important claim.
4. **Connect** it to the wider ecosystem with evidence.
5. **Check boundaries** around rights, consent, affiliation, and endorsement.
6. **Pause safely** with one clear return point.

---

# Capture first. Classify later.

The minimum useful record asks only:

1. What is this?
2. Where did it come from?

Everything else may be `unknown`, `not reviewed`, or `return later`.

Speaker note: Demonstrate saving one source without choosing a folder or completing every field.

---

# Labels prevent accidental certainty

| Label | What it communicates |
| --- | --- |
| Verified | Direct evidence supports it |
| Creator-stated | The creator says it |
| Third-party claim | A named outside source says it |
| Interpretation | It explains meaning or function |
| Inference | Evidence suggests it |
| Unresolved | We cannot establish it yet |

---

# Connection is not proof

A source can be:

- historically connected without proving creative intent;
- thematically related without proving influence;
- publicly available without granting permission;
- discussed beside someone without establishing collaboration;
- part of a proof of concept without being commercially cleared.

Speaker note: Use your community's example here. Show both the connection and its boundary.

---

# A neurodivergent-safe return loop

At every pause, preserve:

- what is safely captured;
- what was confirmed;
- what remains unresolved;
- the last completed action;
- one recommended next action.

Suggested return message:

> Three sources are safely preserved. One authorship question remains. Continue there or capture something new.
